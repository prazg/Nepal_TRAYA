## 🇳🇵 Nepal TRĀYA — Nepal Multi-Hazard Early Warning System

TRĀYA (त्राय) derives from the Sanskrit verbal root √trā, meaning to protect, save, or deliver. It represents an open, integrated platform for multi-hazard monitoring, prediction, early warning, and risk communication across Nepal.

## Detect. Warn. Protect.

A screening assessment of glacial lake outburst flood, earthquake and
earthquake-triggered landslide hazard for Nepal, built entirely from published
methods and open data, with live feeds read by the browser.

**This is a screening and prioritisation tool, not a forecast and not an
operational warning service.** Read the "Methods and limits" tab before using
any number from it.

## What it does

For each of the 1,429 mapped glacial lakes in Nepal:

| step | question | method |
|---|---|---|
| mass movement | can an avalanche or rockfall reach the lake? | slope thresholds and reach angle over a D8 route on a depression-filled Copernicus DEM GLO-30 — Rounce et al. (2016), after Alean (1985), Bolch et al. (2011), Huggel et al. (2004) |
| dam stability | can the moraine fail on its own? | steep lakefront area, 10° depression angle within 1 km — Fujita et al. (2013) |
| water | how much could be released? | depth–area posterior on 24 Himalayan bathymetric surveys and a half-ellipsoid basin — Veh et al. (2020); independently, potential flood volume from the lowering that removes the steep lakefront — Fujita et al. (2013) |
| flood | how big a peak? | dimensionless peak-discharge relation fitted piecewise to 63 natural dam breaks — Walder & O'Connor (1997), O'Connor & Beebee (2009), as formulated by Veh et al. (2020) |
| downstream | who is in the way, and when? | routing along HydroRIVERS, exposure from OpenStreetMap, population from WorldPop, travel times from a bracket of surge-front velocities |
| classification | how do these combine? | hazard, downstream impact and risk classes of Rounce et al. (2016) |

Live layers, fetched by the browser and stored nowhere: USGS real-time
earthquake feeds, USGS ShakeMap MMI contours, the USGS ground-failure product
(its operational run of the Nowicki Jessee et al. 2018 coseismic landslide
model), and Open-Meteo precipitation on request.

### Basemaps

No API key is needed for any of them, and the tool works with none at all.
CARTO's basemaps, used in an earlier version, began requiring a key and now
return a watermarked tile, so they were removed. The switcher offers:

| basemap | source | licence |
|---|---|---|
| Dark grey (default) | Esri World Dark Gray Base | Esri terms of use, free with attribution |
| Terrain (hillshade) | Esri World Hillshade | Esri terms of use, free with attribution |
| Satellite imagery | Esri World Imagery | Esri terms of use, free with attribution |
| OpenTopoMap | opentopomap.org | map style CC-BY-SA 3.0, data ODbL |
| OpenStreetMap | tile.openstreetmap.org | ODbL, subject to the OSMF tile usage policy |
| No basemap | — | — |

The Esri layers are the closest visual replacement for the old CARTO dark style
and are widely used in research, but their terms should be confirmed before any
high-traffic or commercial deployment; OpenTopoMap and OpenStreetMap are the
unambiguously licensed alternatives. The choice is remembered in the viewer's own
browser, and if a provider starts failing the header says so instead of leaving a
blank map.

## Headline results

- 16 lakes exceed the 10 million m³ potential flood volume that Fujita et al.
  (2013) propose as the screening threshold for detailed investigation. Tsho
  Rolpa is the largest at about 104 million m³.
- 697 lakes have a routed flood path with mapped exposure on it.
- 24 of 24 validation and consistency checks pass, including 8/8 agreement with
  the published avalanche classification of Rounce et al. (2016).


## Validation

Everything in the Validation tab is generated by `src/validate.py` and no
parameter is tuned to improve agreement.

- **Rounce et al. (2016) Table 5**, eight lakes: avalanche 8/8, rockfall 7/8,
  steep-lakefront threshold 7/8.
- **Fujita et al. (2013) Table 1**: our mean-depth and potential-flood-volume
  equations reproduce all five published values.
- **Fujita et al. (2013) Table 2**, nineteen Nepalese lakes: 9/9 of the zero
  values agree, 5/6 of the values above 5 million m³ agree within a factor of
  three. Below 5 million m³ we return zero where they do not — see limitations.
- **Measured values**: Imja Tsho's surveyed maximum depth and the reconstructed
  peak discharge of the 1985 Dig Tsho flood both fall inside our intervals.
- **Dig Tsho after 1985**: the steep lakefront that Fujita et al. report
  disappearing after an outburst is indeed absent from the modern DEM.
- **Unit tests** (`src/test_pipeline.py`): the 4x4 risk matrix, both readings of
  the hazard decision tree, the ellipsoid-cap volume algebra at both limits, the
  dimensionless scaling of the discharge model, slope on synthetic surfaces, D8
  routing and depression filling, and the tag parsers.
- **Sensitivity** (`src/sensitivity.py`): the results are insensitive to the DEM
  window (6-10 km) and the noise-suppression rule, moderately sensitive to the
  steep-lakefront threshold angle, and highly sensitive to which reading of the
  published decision tree is implemented (68% of lakes change class).

## Known limitations, in order of importance

1. **Nothing here is a probability.** No estimate is made of how likely a lake
   is to fail, in any period.
2. **Small potential flood volumes are missed.** A 30 m DEM does not resolve
   marginal steep lakefronts that a 15 m DEM picks up. A potential flood volume
   of zero is a statement about the DEM, not about safety.
3. **Peak discharge carries a factor-of-two typical error** and a 95% interval
   spanning roughly two orders of magnitude. Use the interval.
4. **No inundation footprint is computed.** The 1 km corridor is a search radius
   for exposure, not a flood extent.
5. **Buried ice in moraines** is taken only where the literature documents it,
   so hazard classes are a lower bound elsewhere.
6. **Almost every lake classes as very high hazard**, because the published
   binary framework was designed for eight large suspect lakes and Himalayan
   terrain trips both of its branches nearly everywhere. Prioritise on potential
   flood volume and downstream population instead.
7. **Potential flood volume is close to an area scaling.** For 84% of lakes with
   a steep lakefront the area-depth cap binds, so PFV reduces to 55A^1.25 and the
   discriminating power sits in the binary steep-lakefront test rather than in
   the volume calculation.
8. **OpenStreetMap coverage is uneven**; exposure counts are lower bounds.
9. **Exposure stops at the Nepalese border**, though several routed rivers cross
    into India within the routing distance.

## Running the pipeline

```bash
cd src
python3 fetch_inputs.py --core     # lakes, glaciers, rivers, population (~100 MB)
python3 fetch_inputs.py --dem      # Copernicus DEM tiles (~1.4 GB), only needed
                                   # to regenerate cache/terrain.json
python3 build_terrain.py --workers 4   # per-lake DEM analysis, resumable
python3 exposure.py                    # OpenStreetMap, cached
python3 seismic.py                     # USGS catalogue, cached
python3 test_pipeline.py               # unit tests
python3 build.py                       # assemble data/ and index.html
python3 validate.py                    # 24 checks; non-zero exit on failure
python3 sensitivity.py                 # ~30 min; needs the DEM
python3 verify_refs.py                 # check the bibliography against the DOIs
cd ../paper && make                    # build the manuscript
```

`cache/terrain.json` is committed, so a routine refresh does not need the DEM.
Outputs are byte-deterministic apart from the build timestamp: rerunning
`build.py` on the same inputs produces identical files.

ICIMOD's HMAGLOFDB is not redistributed here. Place `HMAGLOFDB_v4.csv` in
`raw/` to populate the recorded-GLOF layer; the build runs without it.

```

data/*.js               window.LAKES, ROUTES, EXPOSURE, EQ_HISTORY,
                        GLOF_HISTORY, VALIDATION, SUMMARY

vendor/leaflet/         Leaflet 1.9.4, vendored
src/config.py           every threshold, each with its citation
src/terrain.py          DEM analysis: slope, routing, reach angle, SLA, PFV

src/hazard.py           hazard, impact and risk classification
src/build.py            assembly
src/validate.py         validation and consistency checks
.github/workflows/      scheduled refresh
```
